Microphone and delay

With Bluetooth headphones, choose the phone’s own microphone.
More info The headset’s own microphone switches it into phone-call mode: the sound gets quiet and thin, and the volume can’t be changed.
More infoWhy not the headset’s microphone? Bluetooth headphones have two modes. Music mode gives full stereo sound but the microphone is off. Call mode turns the microphone on, but the sound drops to thin mono, gets quiet and the volume can’t be changed. No app can get around this, so Part Coach listens through the phone’s own microphone and the headphones stay in music mode. That works well: the backing plays only in your ears, so the phone hears just your voice, and Measure delay makes up for the Bluetooth delay.
Want a microphone closer to your mouth?
  • Wired or USB-C headset with a microphone: full sound and the headset microphone at the same time, with almost no delay. The most reliable choice.
  • Small clip-on USB-C microphone: keep your Bluetooth headphones for listening and plug the microphone into the phone. Good in a noisy room or when the phone is on a music stand.
  • Newer “LE Audio” earbuds with a recent Android phone can send microphone and good sound at the same time, but it depends on the phone, the earbuds and the browser, so it may not work here.

Display and scoring

8 s
How the colours and scores work
  • Your line is green within 50 cents of the target, amber within a semitone and red further off. The faint blue line is your previous loop pass.
  • Score counts only moments where both the target and you have a clear pitch.
  • On time: at each note start the app checks when you reached the new note; within 80 ms counts as on time. With gentle marks, a small green dot means on time and a faint ring means early or late. Detailed marks add colours and the offset in ms (+ late, − early). The summary after singing tells you if you tend to be early or late.
  • Turn the guide voice down once you know the part, and sing it alone over the backing.
  • You can turn the screen off while singing. The song, scoring and recording keep going, and lock-screen controls can pause it.
